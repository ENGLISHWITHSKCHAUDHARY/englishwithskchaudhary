6. Answer any three of the following in about 100-120 words:

a) Write the summary of any one of the following poems:
i) Now The Leaves Are Falling Fast:
This poem is composed by W.H. Auden. It presents the frustration, loneliness, and decay inherent in human life. The falling leaves symbolize the fast approach of old age and death. Human beings feel isolated as their desires remain unfulfilled. The poet uses imagery like whispering neighbours, freezing arms, and lifeless trees to show how death ends human activity, leaving us silent like the nightingale.
ii) Fire Hymn:
This poem is composed by Keki N. Daruwalla. It describes a ghastly scene at a burning ghat where half-burnt human body parts are seen. As a Zoroastrian (Parsi), the poet's religion forbids cremation and prefers disposing of bodies in the Tower of Silence. However, due to distance, the poet once consigned his firstborn child to the flames. The poem reflects his deep guilt and repentance for violating his religious customs.
iii) The Soldier:
This poem is composed by Rupert Brooke. It is a patriotic war sonnet in which the speaker expresses his profound love and devotion for his motherland, England. He states that if he dies in a foreign battlefield, that piece of earth will become forever England because a son nurtured by English air, soil, and rivers lies buried there. The poem conveys intense patriotism and peace in death.
b) Write the summary of any one of the following prose-pieces:
i) The Artist:
Written by Shiga Naoya, this story revolves around a Japanese boy named Seibei who is passionately interested in collecting and polishing gourds. His talent is suppressed when a school teacher catches him polishing a gourd in class. The teacher confounds his father, who smashes all of Seibei's collected gourds. Later, the confiscated gourd is sold to a curio-dealer for 50 yen, highlighting how traditional society discourages natural artistic talent.
ii) A Child is Born:
Written by Germaine Greer, this essay contrasts traditional child-rearing practices with modern Western methods. In traditional societies, childbirth is a joint family and community celebration where emotional support is given to the mother. Though modern Western medicine has reduced mortality rates, it has stripped the mother of cultural and emotional comfort, making childbirth an isolated, medicalized procedure.
iii) India Through a Traveller's Eyes:
Written by Pearl S. Buck, this essay presents her warm admiration for Indian life, culture, and hospitality. She praises the wisdom, devotion, and peace of Indian peasants as well as the idealism of the educated youth. She reflects on how Indian simplicity, religious tolerance, and strong values form the core backbone of its national character.
c) Write a note on Middle English dialects / Future of English / American English:
i) Write a note on the dialects of Middle English:
Middle English (1150–1500 AD) had five main regional dialects: Northern, Southern, East Midland, West Midland, and Kentish. Among these, the East Midland dialect gained prominence because it was used in London and nearby educational hubs like Oxford and Cambridge. It eventually developed into Standard Literary English.
ii) Write a note on the future of English:
English has become a Global Language and the main medium for international trade, science, diplomacy, technology, and internet communication. With the rapid spread of digital communication and globalization, English will continue to expand worldwide, accommodating regional variations while maintaining its position as a universal lingua franca.
iii) Write a note on American English:
American English began developing after British settlement in America in the 17th century. Noah Webster standardized it through his dictionary, introducing distinct spelling variations (e.g., 'color' instead of 'colour', 'center' instead of 'centre'). Today, American English dominates worldwide media, technology, and business communication.
