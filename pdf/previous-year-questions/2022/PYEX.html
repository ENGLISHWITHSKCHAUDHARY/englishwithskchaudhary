6. Answer any three of the following in about 100-120 words:

a) Write the summary of any one of the following poems:

(i) Now The Leaves Are Falling Fast
'Now The Leaves Are Falling Fast' is composed by W. H. Auden. In this poem, the poet depicts the frustration, suffering, and loneliness inherent in human life. The falling leaves symbolize the fast decay of life and the arrival of old age leading to death. The whispering neighbours distract human beings from real happiness, while the pram rolls on towards the grave. Despite the despair, the poet points out a ray of hope represented by the freezing mountain waterfall that blesses travelers in their last distress.


(ii) Fire Hymn
'Fire Hymn' is a powerful poem written by Keki N. Daruwalla. The poem describes a burning ghat scene where the poet witnesses half-burnt limbs of corpses left by the fire, violating its sacred duty. Being a Parsi, the poet's religion forbids cremation and prescribes disposing of dead bodies in the Tower of Silence. However, twenty years earlier, he was forced to cremate his first-born child because the Tower of Silence was a thousand miles away. The poet expresses his grief and guilt, taking a vow to save the fire from the sin of forgetfulness.


(iii) The Soldier
'The Soldier' is a patriotic sonnet composed by Rupert Brooke during World War I. The poet expresses his deep love, gratitude, and devotion towards his motherland, England. He asserts that if he dies on a foreign battlefield, that piece of land will forever belong to England, as a dust shaped and nurtured by English air, sun, and flowers lies buried there. The poem reflects a noble, idealist vision of patriotism, expressing that his soul will return all thoughts, peace, and laughter back to an English heaven.

b) Write the summary of any one of the following prose-pieces:

(i) The Artist
'The Artist' is a touching short story written by Shiga Naoya. It revolves around a twelve-year-old Japanese schoolboy named Seibei, who possesses an extraordinary passion for collecting and polishing gourds. While his father and teacher want him to focus strictly on academic studies, Seibei finds artistic fulfillment in crafting unique gourds. One day, a teacher catches him polishing a gourd in class, confiscates it, and complains to his father, who destroys all of Seibei's gourds. Despite this suppression, Seibei shifts his artistic passion to painting pictures, reflecting the societal destruction of childhood talent.


(ii) A Child is Born
'A Child is Born' is an insightful extract by Germaine Greer. The essay explores the traditional cultural practices surrounding childbirth, pregnancy, and child-rearing in non-Western societies compared to modern Western nations. In traditional societies, childbirth is a collective social event where the expectant mother receives immense emotional support, care, and respect from her family and community. In contrast, modern Western medicalization isolates the mother. The essay highlights how traditional customs foster strong familial bonds and provide security to the newborn child.


(iii) India Through a Traveller's Eyes
'India Through a Traveller's Eyes' is an affectionate essay by Pearl S. Buck. She presents her deep admiration for India, its diverse culture, and its people, based on her personal visits. She describes two distinct groups: the young intellectuals in cities who were eager for freedom and Indian peasants in villages who lived simple, pious, and selfless lives. She praises the warmth, hospitality, religious tolerance, and moral dignity of the Indian peasantry, expressing her belief that India's spiritual values hold immense significance for humanity.

c) Write a note on the dialects of Middle English / Future of English / American English:

Dialects of Middle English: Middle English (1150–1500 AD) evolved into five distinct regional dialects: Northern, Southern, East Midlands, West Midlands, and Kentish. Among these, the East Midlands dialect gained prominence because it was used in London, the seat of government, commercial centers, and universities (Oxford and Cambridge). Great literary works, such as Geoffrey Chaucer's The Canterbury Tales, were written in East Midlands, which eventually laid the foundation for Modern Standard English.


OR


Future of English: English has attained the status of a global language, serving as the primary medium for international trade, diplomacy, science, technology, and global communication. The future of English is exceptionally bright, with non-native speakers now outnumbering native speakers. It continues to expand as a global lingua franca while giving rise to diverse regional varieties known as "World Englishes" (such as Indian English, Nigerian English, and Singaporean English), ensuring its dominance in the digital age.


OR


American English: American English began to take its distinct form after British settlers colonized North America in the 17th century. Influenced by Native American languages and immigrants from across Europe, it developed unique features in spelling, pronunciation, and vocabulary. Noah Webster played a pivotal role by publishing American dictionaries that simplified spellings (e.g., 'color' instead of 'colour', 'center' instead of 'centre'). Today, through American cinema, media, and technology, American English exerts global influence.
