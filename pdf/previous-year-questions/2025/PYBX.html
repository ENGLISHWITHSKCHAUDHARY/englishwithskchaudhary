Section-B Solutions: Questions 6 & 7

Complete Solutions for Summaries, Story of English, Matching, Translation, Passage & Precis

6. Answer any three of the following in about 100-120 words each:

a) Write the summary of any one of the following poems:
(i) My Grandmother's House: Composed by Kamala Das, this poem reflects her nostalgic memories of her ancestral home. She received immense, unconditional love from her grandmother there. After her grandmother's death, the house fell silent, and snakes moved among books. The poet now yearns to return to that house to feel the warmth of that lost love.

(ii) Ode to Autumn: Composed by John Keats, this poem celebrates the beauty and abundance of the autumn season. Autumn is depicted as a close friend of the maturing sun, filling trees with ripe fruits and harvesting crops. The poet highlights that autumn has its own sweet music, such as the buzzing of bees, chirping of crickets, and songs of birds.

(iii) Snake: Composed by D.H. Lawrence, the poem describes a soft, golden snake visiting the poet's water trough on a hot day. Although the poet admires the creature, his social education urges him to hit it. After throwing a log at the retreating snake, he immediately regrets his cowardly action and feels guilty for dishonoring a noble creature of nature.
b) Write the summary of any one of the following prose-pieces:
(i) The Artist: Written by Shiga Naoya, this story revolves around a talented Japanese boy named Sebei, who has a passion for collecting and polishing gourds. His passion is misunderstood by his teacher and father, who destroy his collection and force him to give up art. The story highlights how traditional education systems often suppress artistic talent in children.

(ii) How Free is the Press: Written by Dorothy L. Sayers, this essay critically analyzes press freedom. While the press is free from government censorship in peacetime, it is heavily controlled by financial interests, advertisers, and wealthy proprietors. She emphasizes that a truly free press must present honest, unbiased news to the public.

(iii) Indian Civilization and Culture: Written by Mahatma Gandhi, this essay compares ancient Indian civilization with modern Western civilization. Gandhi Ji highlights that Indian civilization promotes moral values, spiritual well-being, and self-restraint, whereas Western civilization promotes materialism and physical luxury. He advises Indians to preserve their rich heritage.
c) Story of English / Notes:
Option 1: Write a note on the dialects of Middle English.
Middle English (AD 1150 – 1500) developed five distinct dialects across different geographical regions of England: Northern, Southern, East Midland, West Midland, and Kentish. Among these, the East Midland dialect gained prominence because it was used in London and supported by major writers like Geoffrey Chaucer, eventually forming the basis of Modern Standard English.

Option 2: Write a note on English as a second language of India.
English plays a vital role in India as an official second language. It serves as a bridge for administrative, educational, scientific, and commercial communication across states with diverse native languages. Learning English opens up global career opportunities and keeps Indian students connected with modern international advancements.

Option 3: Write a note on American English.
American English began evolving after British settlers migrated to America in the 17th century. Over time, it developed unique spellings (e.g., 'color' instead of 'colour'), distinct vocabulary (e.g., 'elevator' for 'lift'), and pronunciation patterns influenced by diverse immigrant communities. Today, American English dominates global media, technology, and trade.

7. Read the passage carefully and answer the questions OR Write a Precis:

Option 1: Read the passage carefully and answer the questions that follow:
Passage Text: The smile is the best tonic for our mind and body. It takes thirteen muscles to smile, but forty-three to be angry. To be cheerful, we have to create positive thoughts. On the other hand, if we constantly think about negative things, we feel unhappy. So the best way to avoid a negative idea is to replace it with a positive one. When we are relaxed and in bed, we should practise putting some cheerful thoughts.
Questions & Answers:

a) What is the best tonic for our mind and body?
Answer: The smile is the best tonic for our mind and body.

b) What should we practise when we are relaxed in bed?
Answer: When we are relaxed and in bed, we should practise putting cheerful thoughts into our mind.

c) What should we do to be cheerful?
Answer: To be cheerful, we have to create positive thoughts and replace negative ideas with positive ones.

d) What happens when we constantly think about negative things?
Answer: When we constantly think about negative things, we feel unhappy.
OR Option 2: Write a précis of the passage and give a suitable title:
Original Passage Text: The character of good men is like that of trees. They live for others and do not lose their qualities even in losing their lives. They always follow the right path. Praise is immaterial to them. To live for one's own sake is to lead the life of beasts. Only those who lay down their lives for others will live forever in a world of bliss.
Title: Ideal Character of Good Men

Précis Answer: Good men resemble trees because they live selflessly for others and adhere to righteousness regardless of praise. Selfish living is like that of beasts, whereas sacrificing for others grants eternal bliss.
