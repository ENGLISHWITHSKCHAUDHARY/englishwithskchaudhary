Section-B Solutions: Questions 1 to 5

Complete Coverage of Every Single Sub-part (a to j)

1. Write an essay on any one of the following in about 150-200 words:

a) Clean India Movement
The Clean India Movement, or Swachh Bharat Abhiyan, was launched to fulfill Mahatma Gandhi's vision of a clean nation. It aims to eliminate open defecation, improve waste management, and spread public health awareness. Sanitation directly prevents diseases like malaria and cholera. Citizens and students must actively participate to make our country clean and healthy.
b) Climate Change
Climate change is a major global threat caused by deforestation, industrial pollution, and burning fossil fuels. It causes rising global temperatures, melting glaciers, and extreme weather events. To combat this issue, we must reduce greenhouse emissions, plant more trees, and shift toward renewable energy sources like solar and wind power.
c) Social Justice
Social justice means equal rights, opportunity, and treatment for all individuals regardless of their caste, religion, gender, or economic status. It aims to remove social inequalities and protect marginalized communities. Promoting education, fair laws, and equal economic opportunities are essential steps to achieving social justice in a democratic nation.
d) Education for Everyone
Education is the fundamental right of every human being and the foundation of nation-building. It empowers individuals with knowledge, reduces poverty, and opens up economic opportunities. Ensuring free and compulsory education for all children, especially in rural areas, helps eliminate social discrimination and guarantees national development.
e) Bihar of your Dreams
The Bihar of my dreams is a progressive, prosperous, and peaceful state known for quality education and employment. It has top-class infrastructure, clean cities, vibrant agriculture, and thriving industries. In this Bihar, every youth gets quality employment locally, law and order prevail, and rich historical heritage blends seamlessly with modern technology.

2. Explain any one of the following lines (Prose):

a) "I have a dream that my four children will one day live in a nation where they will not be judged by the colour of their skin but by the content of their character."
Answer: Taken from Martin Luther King, Jr.'s speech I Have a Dream[span_0](start_span)[span_0](end_span). The speaker envisions a society free from racial discrimination where people are judged purely by their moral integrity, character, and values rather than their skin color or race.
b) "Justice was tolerably fair. The ordinary rule was to avoid courts."
Answer: Taken from Mahatma Gandhi's essay Indian Civilization and Culture. Gandhi Ji explains that ancient Indian village administration maintained fair justice, and common people preferred settling disputes locally rather than getting trapped in expensive court litigation.
c) "You two'd better hurry up and get married... Have me in peace."
Answer: Taken from Anton Chekhov's play A Marriage Proposal. Choobookov, the father of Natalya, says this to Lomov and Natalya out of exasperation so that their endless petty arguments end and they finally get married.
d) "I went to India to see and listen to two groups of people, the young intellectuals in the cities and the peasants in the villages."
Answer: Taken from Pearl S. Buck's travelogue India Through a Traveller's Eye. The author highlights her purpose of visiting India—to understand its independence movement through city intellectuals and rural farmers.

3. Explain any one of the following lines (Poetry):

a) "The voice of my education said to me / He must be killed"
Answer: Taken from D.H. Lawrence's poem Snake[span_1](start_span)[span_1](end_span). The poet experiences an inner conflict where his social education tells him to kill the golden venomous snake, while his human instinct admires its beauty[span_2](start_span)[span_2](end_span).
b) "Can you, that I lived in such a house and / Was proud, and loved..............."
Answer: Taken from Kamala Das's poem My Grandmother's House. The poet reminisces about her happy childhood spent at her grandmother's house, where she felt immense pride and received unconditional love.
c) "There shall be / In that rich earth a richer dust concealed; / A dust whom England bore, shaped, made aware"
Answer: Taken from Rupert Brooke's sonnet The Soldier[span_3](start_span)[span_3](end_span). The soldier expresses patriotism, stating that if he dies fighting abroad, his body—nurtured by England—will make that foreign earth forever English[span_4](start_span)[span_4](end_span).
d) "Nurses to the graves are gone, / And the prams go rolling on."
Answer: Taken from W.H. Auden's poem Now the Leaves are Falling Fast[span_5](start_span)[span_5](end_span). The poet reflects on the continuous cycle of life and death, showing that older generations die while new life continues to move forward.

5. Answer any five of the following in about 40-50 words each:

a) What, according to Gandhiji, is the 'sheet anchor of our hope'?
Answer: According to Gandhiji, Indian civilization and its moral values are the 'sheet anchor of our hope[span_12](start_span)'[span_12](end_span). Unlike Western civilization, which focuses on material needs, Indian culture prioritizes moral duty and self-restraint[span_13](start_span)[span_13](end_span).
b) What is the business of education?
Answer: According to Dr. Zakir Hussain, the primary business of education is nation-building, developing moral character, and continuously renewing human culture and social growth.
c) What are the 'trials and tribulations' that Martin Luther King, Jr. talks about?
Answer: Martin Luther King, Jr. refers to the severe hardships, police brutality, discrimination, and racial persecution faced by African Americans during their civil rights struggle[span_14](start_span)[span_14](end_span).
d) What do free people take for granted?
Answer: According to Dorothy L. Sayers, free people take freedom of the press and speech for granted, often failing to recognize how public opinion can be secretly manipulated.
e) Who are the 'whispering neighbours'?
Answer: In W.H. Auden's poem, 'whispering neighbours' represent the agents of death that isolate human beings from real happiness and drag them toward mortality[span_15](start_span)[span_15](end_span).
f) What does the speaker, in 'Song of Myself', observe in summer?
Answer: In Song of Myself, the speaker Walt Whitman leans and loiters at ease observing a spear of summer grass, connecting deeply with nature's beauty.
g) How did the snake drink water?
Answer: The snake rested its throat on the stone bottom of the trough and silently sipped water through its straight mouth into its long body[span_16](start_span)[span_16](end_span).
h) In what sense does the fire forget its dead?
Answer: Fire 'forgets its dead' when it leaves bodies half-burnt instead of completely consuming them, which the poet considers a sin of forgetfulness[span_17](start_span)[span_17](end_span).
i) Write a note on the development of Indian poetry in English.
Answer: Indian English poetry started in the 19th century with pioneers like Henry Derozio, Toru Dutt, and Rabindranath Tagore, reflecting Indian culture, freedom struggles, and modern themes.
j) Who were known as the University Wits?
Answer: University Wits were a group of late 16th-century English playwrights educated at Oxford or Cambridge, including Christopher Marlowe, Thomas Kyd, and Robert Greene.
