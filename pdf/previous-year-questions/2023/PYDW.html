1. Write an essay on any one of the following in about 150-200 words:

a) Newspaper
A newspaper is a powerful tool of communication that keeps us informed about current events, sports, politics, and world affairs. It plays a crucial role in a democracy by forming public opinion and holding the government accountable. Daily reading enhances vocabulary and broadens knowledge. Despite the rise of digital media, newspapers remain an essential source of authentic news for citizens.
b) National Integration
National integration is the bond of unity that connects the citizens of a nation regardless of religion, language, region, or caste. In a diverse country like India, it is vital for maintaining peace, stability, and national progress. Education, mutual respect, and cultural exchange help strengthen national harmony and protect our country against communal disharmony.
c) Pollution
Pollution is one of the most serious environmental threats facing the world today. Air, water, and soil pollution are caused by industrial waste, vehicle emissions, and excessive plastic use. It leads to severe health conditions such as respiratory diseases and global warming. To control pollution, we must promote tree plantation, proper waste management, and sustainable clean energy sources.
d) Health is Wealth
The famous proverb "Health is Wealth" reminds us that good health is far more valuable than material possessions. A healthy mind and body enable a person to lead a fulfilling and productive life. Balanced nutrition, regular exercise, proper sleep, and mental wellness are necessary to maintain good health. Without health, no amount of wealth can bring true happiness.
e) Discipline
Discipline is the practice of training one's mind and body to follow rules and order. It is the key to personal and professional success. In a student's life, discipline helps in managing time, maintaining focus, and achieving academic goals. A disciplined society functions smoothly, whereas a lack of discipline leads to chaos, inefficiency, and failure.

2. Explain any one of the following lines (Prose):

a) "In no civilization have all men attained perfection."
Answer: Taken from Mahatma Gandhi's essay Indian Civilization and Culture. Gandhi Ji highlights that human weaknesses exist in every society, but the uniqueness of Indian civilization lies in its foundation of moral integrity and self-restraint rather than physical indulgence.
b) "There is a material difference between the concept of education in the East and in the West."
Answer: Taken from Dr. Zakir Hussain's speech Bharat Is My Home. The speaker explains that Western education often focuses on individual material growth, whereas Eastern philosophy emphasizes spiritual, moral, and social responsibilities for the collective good.
c) "I am a land-grabber, that's what I am."
Answer: Taken from Anton Chekhov's play A Marriage Proposal. Lomov makes this dramatic self-accusatory remark during a fiery argument with Natalya and Choobookov over the ownership of Oxen Meadows to show his frustration.
d) "Religion is ever-present in Indian life, in its best aspects as well as in its worst."
Answer: Taken from Pearl S. Buck's travelogue India Through a Traveller's Eye. The author observes that spiritual values guide the everyday conduct and hospitality of Indians, though religious devotion can sometimes be misguided.

3. Explain any one of the following lines (Poetry):

a) "He lifted his head from his drinking, as cattle do"
Answer: Taken from D.H. Lawrence's poem Snake[span_1](start_span)[span_1](end_span). The poet describes the calm and innocent behavior of the snake at his water trough, comparing its movement to harmless farm animals.
b) "Cold, impossible, ahead / Lifts the mountain's lovely head"
Answer: Taken from W.H. Auden's poem Now the Leaves Are Falling Fast[span_5](start_span)[span_5](end_span). The poet uses the image of cold mountain peaks to symbolize distant ultimate peace and spiritual rest after the struggles of human life.
c) "I think she was the most beautiful lady / That ever was in the West Country"
Answer: Taken from Walter de la Mare's poem An Epitaph. The poet expresses his grief over the death of a beloved lady, reflecting on how physical beauty is temporary and fades away with time.
d) "There is a house now far away where once I received love"
Answer: Taken from Kamala Das's poem My Grandmother's House. The poet nostalgically remembers her childhood spent with her grandmother, where she experienced unconditional love that she now seeks elsewhere.

5. Answer any five of the following in about 40-50 words each:

a) How does Mahatma Gandhi define civilization?
Answer: Mahatma Gandhi defines civilization as that mode of conduct which points out to man the path of duty. Performance of duty and observance of morality are convertible terms that guide human behavior.
b) Why is Dr. Zakir Hussain overwhelmed?
Answer: Dr. Zakir Hussain feels deeply overwhelmed by the trust and confidence placed in him by the people of India by electing him to the highest office of the nation as President.
c) What does Nanukaka do while travelling in a second-class carriage on a third-class ticket?
Answer: Nanukaka managed to travel comfortably in a second-class carriage with a third-class ticket by striking an arrangement with the ticket checker and carrying a kitten in a basket.
d) Why does the speaker in 'Song of Myself' celebrate himself?
Answer: Walt Whitman celebrates himself because he believes that every individual is a part of universal nature and shares the same divine essence and soul with all humanity.
e) What is the central idea of the poem 'An Epitaph'?
Answer: The central idea of 'An Epitaph' is that physical beauty is fleeting and temporary. Outer beauty disappears after death, but moral values and loving memories endure.
f) Why did the poet D.H. Lawrence consider himself a second comer?
Answer: The poet considered himself a second comer because the snake had already arrived at the water trough before him to quench its thirst on a hot day[span_16](start_span)[span_16](end_span).
g) How did the fire-hymn ask the poet to forgive it?
Answer: In Keki N. Daruwalla's 'Fire-Hymn', the burning ghat fire leaves bodies half-charred. The poet feels the fire asks for forgiveness for failing to complete its duty of cremation[span_17](start_span)[span_17](end_span).
h) Write a short note on Old English.
Answer: Old English (449 AD – 1066 AD) was the language spoken by Anglo-Saxons. It was heavily influenced by Germanic dialects and Latin, featured complex inflections, and used a runic alphabet.
i) What was the Great Vowel Shift?
Answer: The Great Vowel Shift was a series of major phonetic changes in the English language between 1400 and 1700, where long vowel sounds systematically shifted upward in pronunciation.
j) Name four major dialects of Middle English.
Answer: The major dialects of Middle English were Northern, Southern, East Midlands, and West Midlands.
