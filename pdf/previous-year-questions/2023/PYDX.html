6. Answer any three of the following in about 100-120 words:

a) Write the summary of any one of the following poems:
(i) Song of Myself
'Song of Myself' is composed by the famous American poet Walt Whitman. In this poem, the poet celebrates the spirit of individualism and universal brotherhood. He feels deeply connected with all of humanity and nature. Whitman believes that every human being is made of the same soil and air, sharing the same divine energy. He encourages us to enjoy life freely without being bound by rigid creeds or religious dogmas. For him, nature in its purest form is a source of joy and truth.

(ii) Snake
'Snake' is a celebrated poem by D.H. Lawrence. On a hot summer day, the poet encounters a gold-colored venomous snake drinking water at his trough. The poet feels honored and admires its graceful, majestic presence. However, his rational education urges him to kill the dangerous snake. Succumbing to societal training, he throws a log at it. Instantly, he feels deep guilt and remorse, condemning his petty action and honoring the snake as an uncrowned king.

(iii) Fire-Hymn
'Fire-Hymn' is a powerful poem written by Keki N. Daruwalla. The poet describes the eerie atmosphere of a burning ghat where dead bodies are cremated. Being a Parsi, whose religion consigns bodies to the Tower of Silence, he once had to cremate his firstborn child due to distance. The sight of half-burnt limbs makes him realize that fire sometimes forgets its duty. The poem reflects on human mortality, religious duties, and the poet's vow to save fire from the sin of forgetfulness.
b) Write the summary of any one of the following prose pieces:
(i) Bharat Is My Home
'Bharat Is My Home' is an extract from the inaugural speech of Dr. Zakir Hussain after taking oath as the President of India in 1967. He expresses deep gratitude to the nation and pledges absolute loyalty to India's cultural heritage. He considers the whole of India as his home and its people as his family. Dr. Hussain emphasizes that true education and hard work are essential for individual growth as well as overall national reconstruction.

(ii) Ideas That Have Helped Mankind
'Ideas That Have Helped Mankind' is written by the eminent philosopher Bertrand Russell. In this essay, he traces the evolution of human progress from prehistoric times to modern civilization. The major factors that helped mankind survive and thrive include the invention of language, agriculture, fire, and moral values. Russell highlights that growth in technical knowledge must be balanced with moral wisdom to build a peaceful human society.

(iii) The Artist
'The Artist' is a touching story written by Japanese writer Shiga Naoya. It revolves around Seibei, a twelve-year-old talented boy who has a passion for collecting and polishing gourds. One day, he buys an exquisite symmetrical gourd, but his ethics teacher catches him polishing it in class. The teacher confiscates it, and his father smashes all his gourds. The story highlights how rigid societal and parental expectations often suppress natural artistic talent in children.
c) Write a note on the importance of English as an International Language / Global Language.
English is universally recognized as the world's premier international language. It serves as a vital bridge for global communication, diplomacy, international trade, science, and technology. A vast majority of research papers, internet content, and academic resources are published in English. In today's interconnected global economy, proficiency in English opens up worldwide employment opportunities. For a developing nation like India, English acts as a window to the modern world, making it essential for educational advancement and global unity.
d) Write a short note on English as a World Language / Story of English.
The story of English is a fascinating journey of growth from a local dialect spoken by Germanic tribes (Anglo-Saxons) to a dominant world language. Over centuries, English absorbed vocabulary from Latin, French, Scandinavian, and Greek, which rich history transformed it through Old, Middle, and Modern English phases. Today, English is spoken across continents as a mother tongue, second language, or foreign language. Its flexibility, vast vocabulary, and capacity to adopt words from foreign cultures have turned it into an indispensable medium for worldwide media, trade, and higher education.

7. Read the passage carefully and answer the questions that follow OR Write a Précis:

Option 1: Passage Reading & Questions
Passage:
Education is the most powerful tool for human development. It empowers individuals with knowledge, critical thinking skills, and moral values. Education removes ignorance and opens pathways for career growth and economic security. In a democratic society, educated citizens actively participate in governance and contribute positively to national progress. Therefore, providing quality education to every child is essential for building a prosperous nation.

Questions & Answers:
Q1. What is the most powerful tool for human development?
Ans: Education is the most powerful tool for human development.

Q2. How does education empower individuals?
Ans: Education empowers individuals with knowledge, critical thinking skills, and moral values.

Q3. What role do educated citizens play in a democracy?
Ans: Educated citizens actively participate in governance and contribute positively to national progress.

Q4. Why is quality education essential for a nation?
Ans: Quality education for every child is essential to eliminate ignorance and build a prosperous nation.
Option 2 (OR): Write a Précis of the given passage and give a suitable title
Original Text (approx. 85 words):
Trees are our best friends on earth. They clean the air by absorbing harmful carbon dioxide and releasing vital oxygen. Trees prevent soil erosion, maintain rainfall patterns, and provide shelter to numerous birds and animals. They also supply timber, fruits, and medicinal products essential for human survival. Deforestation leads to environmental destruction and climate instability. Therefore, protecting existing forests and planting new trees is crucial for maintaining life on Earth.

Précis (approx. 28 words):
Trees are vital for ecological balance as they purify air, conserve soil, and support wildlife. Protecting trees is essential to prevent environmental destruction and sustain life.

Suitable Title: Importance of Trees / Protecting Our Green Cover
