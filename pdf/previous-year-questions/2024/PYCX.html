6. Answer any three of the following in about 100-120 words each:

a) Write the summary of any one of the following poems:

(i) Song of Myself
'Song of Myself' is composed by the famous American poet Walt Whitman. In this poem, the poet celebrates the joy of individual existence, human equality, and nature. He asserts that every atom belonging to him belongs to every other human being equally. He invites his soul to observe nature freely without artificial barriers of religion, caste, or creed. Whitman expresses that life is a dynamic, continuous journey and encourages readers to embrace truth, freedom, and universal brotherhood.


(ii) Snake
'Snake' is written by D.H. Lawrence. The poem narrates the speaker's encounter with a golden snake at his water trough on a hot summer day in Sicily. The snake drinks water peacefully and behaves like a noble guest. However, the speaker's modern education urges him to hit the harmless creature. After throwing a log at it, the speaker feels immediate guilt and regret, viewing the snake as an uncrowned king of the underworld and despising human pettiness.


(iii) My Grandmother's House
'My Grandmother's House' is an emotional poem by Kamala Das. The poet fondly recalls her childhood spent in her grandmother's unconditional love and affection. After the grandmother's death, the house became silent, and snakes moved among books. In her adult life, the poet feels a deep void and searches for that pure, unselfish love at strangers' doors, highlighting the poignant contrast between past warmth and present emotional isolation.

b) Write the summary of any one of the following prose pieces:

(i) Indian Civilization and Culture
'Indian Civilization and Culture' is an insightful essay by Mahatma Gandhi. He emphasizes that Indian civilization is rooted in moral elevation, self-restraint, and spiritual values, whereas Western civilization promotes materialism and physical indulgence. Gandhi Ji highlights that while foreign empires perished, Indian heritage survived because of its focus on simple living, high thinking, and community harmony, advising Indians to cherish their ancestral cultural foundation.


(ii) Ideas That Have Helped Mankind
'Ideas That Have Helped Mankind' is authored by Bertrand Russell. The essay analyzes key milestones in human evolution, categorized into intellectual growth and moral progress. Important developments like the invention of speech, the discovery of fire, the domestication of animals, and the introduction of agriculture helped humans rise above animal existence. Russell notes that while intellectual capabilities expanded dramatically, human happiness depends heavily on moral wisdom and international cooperation.


(iii) A Marriage Proposal
'A Marriage Proposal' is a one-act comedy play by Anton Chekhov. It depicts Ivan Lomov arriving at his neighbor Stepan Chubukov's house to propose marriage to his daughter, Natalya. However, trivial disputes over property ownership (Oxen Meadows) and hunting dogs continuously interrupt the proposal. Despite intense arguments, Chubukov forces them to agree, delivering a humorous satire on socio-economic motives behind marriage in aristocratic society.

c) Write a note on Old English / Middle English / Modern English:

Old English: Spoken from 449 AD to 1066 AD, Old English was Germanic in origin, brought by Anglo-Saxon tribes. It featured complex inflections, distinct dialects, and a Latin-influenced alphabet with unique runes.

Middle English: Spoken from 1150 AD to 1500 AD, Middle English emerged after the Norman Conquest (1066). It experienced French vocabulary integration, simplified grammar inflections, and the Great Vowel Shift.

Modern English: Spanning from 1500 AD to the present, Modern English expanded rapidly due to the printing press, the Renaissance, industrialization, and global trade, establishing English as a world lingua franca.

d) Write a note on English as an International Language / Global Language:
English has emerged as the global lingua franca, serving as the primary medium for international trade, diplomacy, science, aviation, higher education, and technology. Its wide reach stems from historical expansion, economic influences, and flexible vocabulary assimilation. Today, non-native English speakers outnumber native speakers, making proficiency in English an essential skill for global career opportunities, digital communication, and cross-cultural exchange.

7. Read the passage and answer the questions that follow OR Write a Précis:

Option 1: Reading Comprehension Passage

"Civilization is that mode of conduct which points out to man the path of duty. Performance of duty and observance of morality are convertible terms. To observe morality is to attain mastery over our minds and our passions. So doing, we know ourselves. The Gujarati equivalent for civilization means 'good conduct'."

Questions & Answers:

Q(a) What is civilization?
Ans: Civilization is that mode of conduct which points out to man the path of duty.

Q(b) What does the Gujarati equivalent for civilization mean?
Ans: The Gujarati equivalent for civilization means 'good conduct'.

Q(c) How can we attain mastery over our minds and passions?
Ans: We can attain mastery over our minds and passions by observing morality and performing our duties.

Q(d) What are convertible terms according to the passage?
Ans: Performance of duty and observance of morality are convertible terms.

Option 2 (OR): Write a Précis of the given passage and give a suitable title

Original Text:
Books are a source of unending joy, wisdom, and knowledge. A good book becomes a lifelong companion that guides an individual through trials and hardship. Reading sharpens intellect, broadens imagination, and refines character. Therefore, cultivating a habit of reading good literature shapes a person into an enlightened citizen.

Title: The Power and Value of Reading

Précis:
Good books provide continuous knowledge, joy, and lifelong companionship. They sharpen intellect, broaden perspective, and refine character, transforming individuals into enlightened citizens.
